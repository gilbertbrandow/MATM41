# MATM41 - Galois Theory

MATM41 Galois Theory, 7.5 credits, is an elective second-cycle course for a Master of Science degree in mathematics. The course is taught in English.

The course aims to provide a deeper understanding of field extensions and the connection between polynomial equations and group theory.

## Course content

The course covers:

- Field extensions: splitting fields, normal extensions, separable extensions, field automorphisms, and normal closures
- Galois groups: Galois extensions, the Galois Correspondence, and the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory
- Polynomial equations: solvability by radicals, insolvable quintics, symmetric polynomials, and cyclotomic extensions

## Learning outcomes

After completing the course, the student should be able to:

- Give a detailed account of the concepts, theorems, and methods included in the course
- Identify the most important theorems and present their proofs
- Integrate knowledge from different parts of the course when solving problems
- Identify problems suited to methods from Galois theory and apply appropriate solution methods
- Explain mathematical solutions coherently in speech and writing using appropriate terminology
- Explain the importance of Galois theory in other areas of mathematics, particularly the theory of polynomial equations

## Assessment

The examination consists of:

- A written examination worth 5 credits
- An oral examination worth 2.5 credits
- The oral examination may only be taken after passing the written examination
- A resit written examination is offered shortly after the ordinary written examination

Both examinations must be passed.

The oral examination lasts approximately 45 minutes and contains roughly four questions from different parts of the course. The questions are primarily theoretical rather than computational. Students are expected to explain the mathematics orally and may use the blackboard, but should avoid spending too much of the limited examination time writing.

The ordinary written examination takes place on 17 December 2026. Most oral examinations take place on 18 and 21 December 2026. Additional oral-examination times will be offered in mid-to-late January 2027, with exact dates to be announced.

## Grading

The grading scale is Fail, Pass, and Pass with distinction.

Pass with distinction requires at least 75% of the combined maximum points from the written and oral examinations. For this calculation, the maximum points available from the written and oral examinations are weighted 3:1.

## Entry requirements

Admission requires:

- English 6/B
- At least 90 credits in pure mathematics
- MATM31 Algebraic Structures, 7.5 credits, or equivalent knowledge

MATP33 Group and Ring Theory, 7.5 credits, is recommended but not required.

The course may not be included in a degree together with MATM21 Specialised Course on Algebraic Structures, 7.5 credits.
